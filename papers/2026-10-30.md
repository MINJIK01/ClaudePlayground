### [Retrieval as Traversal: Value-Indexed Memory for Stateful Long-Context Agents](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40545v1)
- Daniel Strauß, Zhenshan Bing, Alois Knoll
- 2026-10-29 · cs.AI · Matched: semantic · Relevance: 0.85

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Long-context models repeatedly solve closely related retrieval problems. Attention or global retrieval can retain large stores, but each forward pass again searches for what matters. Recurrent compression avoids this search only by deciding earlier what information to preserve. Neither mechanism directly exploits that useful memories at neighbouring retrieval steps may substantially overlap. We propose an alternative in which retrieval is a persistent local traversal through an address space. We separate a memory's content from its address. Utility is defined by the information revealed by retrieving the content, while geometry determines only how cheaply that information can be reached. This leads to a cost-regularised indexing objective that preserves information value inside local neighbourhoods while charging for every memory made locally accessible. Its marginal-value form gives both attractive and exclusion pressures. Thus, complementary memories should be co-retrievable when the additional value exceeds retrieval cost, whereas redundant memories should not occupy the same retrieval set solely because they are individually useful. As a proof of principle, we test only the simplest fixed-geometry instance on MiniGrid Memory. A persistent local traversal head reaches $0.95$ mean return after $0.59$M training steps with a five-token context, compared with $0.94$M for Gaussian absolute addressing and more than $2$M for categorical absolute addressing. The experiment does not test learned geometry or repeated retrieval. Instead, it motivates the hypothesis that stateful retrieval may be easier to learn than stateless. To test the full proposed method further experimentation is necessary.
</details>

### [Don't Lose the Thread: Empowering Long-Horizon LLM Agents with Cognitive Resource Self-Allocation](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40350v1)
- Zheng Zhu, Xiaozhuan Liang, Xi Chen, Bei Yu, Jiaya Jia
- 2026-10-29 · cs.CL · Matched: semantic · Relevance: 0.88

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Agents powered by large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated remarkable progress in solving complex reasoning tasks. However, LLM agents often falter on long-horizon tasks due to cognitive overload, as their working memory becomes cluttered with expanding and irrelevant information, which dilutes their attention and hinders effective planning and reasoning. To mitigate this challenge, we introduce Cognitive Resource Self-Allocation (CORAL), a novel reasoning paradigm that empowers agents to proactively optimize their context. Implemented as an agent-callable working memory management toolset, CORAL allows an agent to checkpoint its task progress and verified facts, and adaptively initiate a new problem-solving episode by resetting cluttered context while preserving only the checkpointed information, enabling it to resume reasoning from a clean state. We further enhance the agent's checkpointing capabilities using a Multi-episode Agentic Reinforced Policy Optimization algorithm. On the GAIA benchmark, CORAL with an 8B model achieves 42.8% average accuracy, with particularly strong gains on the most challenging long-horizon tasks (Level 2 and Level 3). Crucially, these gains come at a substantially lower inference cost: CORAL consumes 6-7$\times$ fewer input tokens than a ReAct agent. It also generalizes to multi-hop Knowledge QA benchmarks and to the considerably harder BrowseComp, where it improves a strong proprietary agent by 8.0 points.

### [Long-Term Agent Memory through Complementary Curation and Consolidation](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39790v1)
- Abhishek Gupta, Dongge Han, Daniel Madrigal Diaz, Wei-Ning Chen, Mayukh Das et al.
- 2026-10-29 · cs.AI, cs.MA · Matched: semantic · Relevance: 0.90

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Long-horizon dialogue agents require effective memory systems to recall facts, events, and relationships from interaction histories. In this work, we propose a hybrid-memory architecture comprising episodic memories and structured relational memories and a consolidation mechanism which preserves efficiency. We conduct an empirical study across two benchmarks: LoCoMo and LongMemEval-S. Our experiments demonstrate that the two memory types are consistently complementary, combining both outperform state-of-the-art memory frameworks and achieves 90.2% accuracy on LoCoMo and 85.5% on LongMemEval-S.

### [Finding Is Not Returning, Returning Is Not Reconstructing: A Multi-Dimensional Evaluation of Agent Social Re-entry](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39781v1)
- Sheng Wan, Cong Ma, Sichen Dong, Qian'Ang Mao, Chenhan Zhang et al.
- 2026-10-29 · cs.AI, cs.SI · Matched: long-term memory · Relevance: 0.88

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

As Large Language Model (LLM) agents increasingly participate in persistent social environments, understanding their long-term social interaction capabilities becomes increasingly important. While existing research has advanced long-term memory and conversational context retrieval, less attention has been paid to whether agents can autonomously re-engage with previously inactive conversations. In particular, remembering a past interaction does not necessarily mean that an agent can identify when to revisit it, decide to return, or reconstruct the context needed for a coherent continuation. To investigate this gap, we introduce agent social re-entry as a multi- dimensional evaluation problem, distinguishing three complementary capabilities: Finding, Returning, and Reconstructing. We develop a shared-snapshot evaluation framework that measures these dimensions independently, allowing us to examine how conversational resurfacing and persistent memory relate to different aspects of social re-entry. Evaluations across six LLM configurations show that resurfacing substantially improves target conversation identification, while persistent memory is associated with higher rates of autonomous target-directed replying. However, neither improvement is accompanied by consistent gains in contextual reconstruction. These results highlight the distinction between accessing previous conversations, choosing to re-engage, and maintaining conversational continuity. Our framework provides a way to evaluate these capabilities separately rather than relying on memory retrieval or conversational response quality alone.
</details>

### [MemSteer: Turning User Histories into Supervision for Agent Memory Optimization](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39276v1)
- Jie Huang, Cuiling Lan, Qize Yu, Xiulian Peng, Yan Lu
- 2026-10-29 · cs.AI · Matched: semantic · Relevance: 0.87

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

An effective agent memory system should adapt to a user's history and task requirements. Human-annotated supervision can guide this adaptation by providing feedback for evaluating and improving memory designs. Yet suitable labels are rarely available for new user histories, and manually constructing QA pairs and supporting evidence is costly. We introduce MemSteer, which turns unlabeled user histories into question-answer (QA) supervision with supporting evidence for memory-design optimization. MemSteer constructs an evidence-grounded answer plan that specifies how facts across related records jointly support a reference answer. The plan guides question generation, while source-grounded auditing and blind answer recovery check the resulting annotations. To study the utility of this supervision, we develop MemSteer-Opt, which combines QA-based design evaluation with evidence-guided failure diagnosis to revise memory store, retrieval, and context construction without updating model parameters. Experiments on ATM-Bench and RHELM show that this supervision is competitive with official-QA-guided optimization. Relative to Base-BM25, MemSteer-Opt improves ATM-Bench Question Score by 13.4 points and RHELM accuracy by 4.1 and 6.6 points without and with external data, respectively. The supervision also benefits two existing optimizers on ATM-Bench, while new-user experiments demonstrate continued design optimization using supervision generated from the target histories.
</details>

### [Context Compaction for World Models](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38980v1)
- Hayder Tirmazi
- 2026-10-28 · cs.AI · Matched: semantic · Relevance: 0.87

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

World models let observers explore and interact with generated worlds. Recent work shows that when observers revisit a region, current models often fail to regenerate it consistently. We view this as a context retrieval problem. LLM agents manage context retrieval at two layers of their stack. At the model layer, the KV cache design determines how much context fits in memory. At the agent harness layer, harnesses such as Claude Code pick the context sent to the LLM. Prior work on world models has mostly focused only on the model layer. In this paper, we make three contributions. First, we build a benchmark to measure world model context retrieval. It generates new worlds for every episode, preventing a model from recalling rooms using training data. It also separates context retrieval from guesses based on nearby frames. Second, we build an agent harness that performs context compaction for world models, which to our knowledge no prior work has studied. Third, we use our benchmark to study four world model designs. Context compaction lets our world models retrieve rooms up to 4 times beyond their context window without retraining. However, world models with sliding-window attention lose most of their context retrieval when dreaming. Therefore, a dreaming world agent benefits more from extending the reach of its world model using context compaction than switching to sliding window attention at the model layer.
</details>

### [HEDGE: Gated Hypergraph Memory for Scalable Multi-Agent Debate](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38902v1)
- Amirhossein Abaskohi, Mahdi Mostajabdaveh, Zirui Zhou
- 2026-10-28 · cs.CL · Matched: semantic · Relevance: 0.87

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Multi-agent debate systems require agents to retain and revisit arguments across multiple rounds, yet limited context windows and unstructured memories make relevant information increasingly difficult to recover. Existing flat and pairwise-graph memories also struggle to capture higher-order relations, including multi-agent agreement, conflicting evidence, and evolving consensus. We introduce HEDGE, a selective memory framework that represents debate trajectories as semantic hypergraphs. Hyperedges connect related claims across agents, while intra-hyperedge relational graphs preserve fine-grained argumentative structure. A learned memory selection module (MSM), trained through supervised learning and reinforcement learning, routes each memory candidate to STORE, BACKUP, or OMIT, retaining useful information while limiting redundancy. Across seven diverse benchmarks, HEDGE consistently outperforms strong single-agent, multi-agent, and memory baselines, improving accuracy by 5.0 points on average over the strongest multi-agent system with GPT-4o-mini. The gains reach 8.2 points on conflict-heavy tasks, while HEDGE reduces memory usage by 48% and the number of debate rounds by 34%.

### [Routed Continual Learning](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38796v1)
- Michael Philip, Mahesh Balakrishnan, David Geraghty, David Mao, Vidhya Venkat et al.
- 2026-10-28 · cs.LG, cs.MA · Matched: semantic · Relevance: 0.86

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Agents deployed at scale repeatedly encounter related problems, and it is natural to let them learn from each other's experience rather than starting over each time. Unfortunately, there is no single way to retain that experience that works well across settings, and no way to know in advance which future problems a given experience will help with. A global history preserves every detail but becomes expensive and mixes together experience that may not belong together; compressing that history into one fixed representation limits the cost but can throw away valuable details. We introduce Routed Continual Learning (Routed CL), an architecture that decides how to partition and retain experience. Past trajectories are grouped into clusters, each of which maintains state using one memory system from a fixed library. Before each run, an LLM-based router either reuses an existing cluster or creates a new one, basing its decision on the problem statement, memory system descriptions, and cluster summaries. We also introduce Context Memory Adapter, which mediates between the memory system and the agentic loop. We evaluate the architecture on a heterogeneous stream that interleaves 301 instances from the six CL-Bench tasks with 97 banking instances from $τ^3$-Bench. Across four language models and five matched rollouts, Routed Continual Learning raises normalized reward over a single global in-context learning (ICL) history by 15-30% of the maximum attainable normalized reward, achieves higher mean normalized reward than ICL-only clustering, and reduces estimated API cost by 65-72% relative to Global ICL. These results support approaching continual learning as a routing problem that chooses both a learning mechanism and a state cluster rather than committing to one representation globally.
</details>

### [Episodic looking - using reinforcement learning to bias perception enhances few-shot and continual learning with protection from forgetting](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38577v1)
- David Rawlinson, Gideon Kowadlo
- 2026-10-28 · cs.LG, cs.AI · Matched: long-term memory · Relevance: 0.80

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

There is a fundamental contradiction between the desire for models that can generalize, and models that can continuously adapt to recent input with changing statistics. These conditions, captured in few-shot and continual learning settings, can bias or disrupt learned representations. This harms generalization to a broader context, leaving the model unable to overcome input variance. This paper proposes a solution based on the Complementary Learning System architecture (CLS), in which a Short-Term Memory (STM) delegates generalization to a slow, statistical Long-Term Memory (LTM). Delegation is achieved by working in a space of robust, generalized perceptions produced by the LTM. With this separation, the STM can focus on memorizing recent input and avoiding interference between memories. We show that on an image classification task, the resulting CLS model is more resistant to forgetting in a domain-incremental continual learning setting than a baseline ResNet LTM, and generalizes more effectively in a few-shot setting than other continual learners. In the continual setting, overall accuracy is slightly worse than existing continual learning models, but with a different mix of performance characteristics. Results analysis suggests that the model is limited by the representations and representational plasticity of the LTM. In future work, we will seek to address this by replaying STM memories to the LTM.
</details>

### [CALM: Conflict-Aware Lifecycle Memory Governance for Long-Horizon Language Agents](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38573v1)
- Chang Li, Xianhao Ou, Xinyan Ma, Hao Li, Haoyuan Xu et al.
- 2026-10-28 · cs.AI · Matched: semantic · Relevance: 0.88

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

A memory can be outdated for present-day reasoning yet indispensable for reconstructing the past. Relevance-based retrieval overlooks this tension: semantic relevance, evidence-grounded validity, and query-specific eligibility are distinct. We propose CALM, a conflict-aware lifecycle memory governance framework that decouples persistent memory preservation from query-time admission. CALM converts heterogeneous records into evidence-grounded memory cells, updates validity and lifecycle states using support, conflict, and dependency signals, and admits query-appropriate evidence through training-free or learned gating informed by temporal applicability. Across LongMemEval-S and LoCoMo with Qwen and GPT backbones, CALM outperforms state-of-the-art baselines, achieving the best overall accuracy. Compared with simple filtering and reranking, query-conditioned admission further improves evidence selection. Conflict-blocking and robustness analyses show CALM suppresses erroneous retrieved memories while retaining useful evidence. Historical-reconstruction experiments demonstrate that preserving superseded records supports accurate earlier-state reasoning without compromising current-state answers.
</details>

### [GAAC: Lossless KV Compression for Bandwidth-Efficient LLM Inference on Personal Devices](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38210v1)
- Yipin Guo, Arun M George, Siddharth Joshi
- 2026-10-28 · cs.AI · Matched: persona + memory · Relevance: 0.79

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Weight-only, lossy quantization substantially reduces the storage and memory requirements for serving large language models (LLMs), but the growing key-value (KV) cache remains a bottleneck for long-context inference on personal devices. We present Group-Adaptive Additive Codec (GAAC), a lossless KV compression and serving system that exploits exponent redundancy to reduce storage and accelerate attention without introducing KV approximation. GAAC encodes BF16 exponents using calibrated per-channel base-plus-offset codes over contiguous ranges. GAAC adapts code widths across small groups while preserving KV values bit-for-bit. A fused attention kernel reconstructs KV tiles on the fly, avoiding full-cache decompression. Block-wise compression and fixed-capacity slots support incremental cache updates and paged serving. Across five tested models, GAAC achieves 1.405-1.407$\times$ lossless BF16 KV compression. We also study compression performance on low-precision tensors FP8 E4M3 caches ( 1.09-1.14$\times$) and E5M2 caches (1.25-1.30$\times$). On NVIDIA DGX Spark, GAAC accelerates decode attention at a 64K-token context by 1.19-1.27$\times$ and end-to-end W4A16 decode steps by 1.04-1.15$\times$. In serving, it reduces resident KV memory by approximately 26% and increases aggregate throughput by 13-29% under comparable KV-memory budgets. On Apple M4, GAAC achieves 1.02-1.25$\times$ attention speedups, demonstrating portability beyond NVIDIA GPUs.

### [SkillMem: Compact External Skill Memory for Latent Multi-Agent Reasoning](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37935v1)
- Yuxuan Zhu, Mohammad Mohammadi Amiri, Pin-Yu Chen, Keerthiram Murugesan
- 2026-10-27 · cs.AI, cs.CL, cs.LG · Matched: semantic · Relevance: 0.89

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Latent multi-agent systems reduce inference cost by keeping intermediate reasoning and inter-agent communication in continuous representations.\ Reusable skills, however, are commonly supplied as text or stored as layer-wise Key-Value states or skill-specific model parameters, introducing repeated processing or substantial per-skill storage.\ We introduce \textbf{SkillMem}, which represents each reusable skill as a small external latent memory and makes it directly available during latent multi-agent reasoning.\ A role-conditioned cross-attention reader allows the same memory to guide different agents according to their roles and current hidden states.\ We train the skill memories and shared reader through behavioral distillation, matching the change in token predictions induced by a textual skill while keeping the backbone frozen.\ For our experiments, we obtain textual skills from paired successful and failed multi-agent trajectories, although existing skills can also be compiled directly.\ Across six benchmarks and Qwen3 models ranging from 4B to 14B parameters, SkillMem provides strong gains for smaller backbones while preserving efficient latent multi-agent inference across model scales.\ We use the same $8\times256$ memory configuration across all evaluated model scales, requiring 9.6\,KB of skill-specific storage per skill, including its retrieval embedding.\ For a library of 100 skills, SkillMem reduces total storage by more than $38\times$ compared with alternative skill representations and achieves up to a $4.23\times$ end-to-end inference speedup.\ These results show that compact procedural memories can be reused without sacrificing the efficiency of latent multi-agent reasoning.
</details>

### [Depth through Recurrence: Towards Ultra-Efficient On-Device ASR](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37833v1)
- Chen Feng, Tianyi Xu, Ramchalam Kinattinkara Ramakrishnan, Yicheng Lin, Shaojie Zhuo et al.
- 2026-10-27 · cs.LG · Matched: persona + memory · Relevance: 0.78

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Modern automatic speech recognition (ASR) systems have achieved remarkable accuracy by scaling model depth and capacity, but at the cost of increased memory, energy, and computational demands. On edge devices where ASR is often most needed, such as watches and glasses, deploying such large models is infeasible due to extremely constrained resources. This raises a fundamental question: can we achieve high representational power in deep ASR models without scaling up parameterization? In this work, we revisit the role of depth and identify layer wise representational dynamics, in which most layers learn functionally similar transformations. Motivated by this insight, we propose a block-recurrent ASR architecture that replaces parameterized depth with a small set of recurrent blocks, each consisting of weight-shared layers, thereby preserving effective depth while drastically reducing model size. Through representation-guided grouping and knowledge distillation, block-recurrent models retain the accuracy of large reference models while using an order of magnitude fewer parameters. Across Open ASR Leaderboard benchmarks, a model with only two shared blocks recovers 97.4% of the accuracy of large models on average. The compact backbone further enables efficient on-device personalization through lightweight MoE-LoRA adaptation, allowing user-specific models to match or surpass the accuracy of general-purpose ASR systems with significantly lower inference cost. By reusing weights across recurrent steps, the proposed architecture reduces memory-access overhead and inference energy consumption. Together, these results show that high quality ASR can be achieved without large parameterization, and that structured recurrence provides a principled path towards ultra-compressed, energy-efficient, and personalized speech recognition on the edge.
</details>
